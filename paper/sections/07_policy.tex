\section{Policy Implications}
\label{sec:policy}

\subsection{The Augmented Taylor Rule: Operational Implementation}

Proposition~3 prescribes two additions to the standard Taylor rule.

\paragraph{Accommodate the fragmentation premium ($\phi_\phi^* = -0.22$).}
When $\hat\phi_t > 0$, the economy faces a supply-side cost-push shock.
The standard rule tightens in response to inflation, further depressing
output without addressing the supply origin. The augmented rule partially
accommodates, trading a modest inflation overshoot ($+0.3$pp peak) for a
substantially smaller output trough ($-0.8$pp vs.\ $-1.4$pp). Operationally,
this coefficient can be conditioned on cross-bloc trade flow divergence,
shipping cost indices, or the composite fragmentation index $F_t$ from
Section~\ref{sec:data}. The welfare gain is 0.03\% at baseline
$\sigma_\phi = 0.05$, rising to $\approx 0.5\%$ at $\sigma_\phi = 0.20$.

\paragraph{Respond to the wage markdown indicator ($\phi_\mu^* \geq 0$).}
Markdown deepening in recession compresses incomes of hand-to-mouth
households, implying the central bank should ease more aggressively during
concentrated-market downturns. At baseline $\gamma = 0.8$, $\phi_\mu^*
\approx 0$; it rises to economically significant magnitudes as markdown
cyclicality or the HtM share increase. Conditioned on HHI data from
\citet{azar2020}, this provides a real-time operational handle on
distributional monetary policy.

\paragraph{Revising the NAIRU.}
The wage-consistent NAIRU lies below the inflation-consistent NAIRU by:
\begin{equation}
  u^*_{\text{price}} - u^*_{\text{wage}} = \frac{\bar\mu^w}{\gamma}
  \left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_p} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_w}\right] \approx 0.4\;\text{pp}
  \label{eq:nairu_gap}
\end{equation}
Central banks using wage-consistent NAIRU estimates may systematically
overtighten, generating unnecessary unemployment.

\subsection{Observable Proxies for Operational Implementation}
\label{sec:observables}

The augmented Taylor rule in Proposition~3 conditions on two variables that
are not directly measured in real time: the fragmentation premium $\hat\phi_t$
and the wage markdown gap $\hat\mu_t^w$. We map each to observable proxies
available to policymakers at high frequency.

\paragraph{Tracking $\hat\phi_t$: fragmentation indicators.}
The composite fragmentation index $F_t$ from Section~\ref{sec:data}---
combining IMF Geoeconomic Fragmentation, PIIE Trade Policy Uncertainty, and
BIS Entity List additions---correlates 0.87 with the structural $\phi_t$
extracted from the model's estimated Armington equations over 2018--2023.
For operational use, we recommend a two-tier approach. As the \emph{primary}
real-time indicator, the cross-bloc merchandise trade intensity from WTO
trade statistics (available monthly with a six-week lag) captures
approximately 70\% of $\phi_t$ variance and is available before the end of
the FOMC meeting cycle. The IMF Geoeconomic Fragmentation Index, released
quarterly, serves as a revision check. Because $\phi_\phi^* = -0.22$ is
calibrated to $\hat\phi_t$ normalized to unit variance, the operational rule
requires scaling by the historical standard deviation of the chosen proxy.
In practice, this means reducing the fed funds rate by approximately 0.5bp
for every 1-point increase in the WTO cross-bloc intensity index (detrended
from its 2015--2019 mean).

\paragraph{Tracking $\hat\mu_t^w$: the markdown gap.}
The structural markdown deviation $\hat\mu_t^w =
[\gamma/(\bar\varepsilon+1)^2]\hat n_t$ is a function of the employment gap
and estimated structural parameters---not directly observable. We propose
two complementary real-time proxies.

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \emph{Online posting HHI (Lightcast/Burning Glass).}  The
    occupation-by-local-market HHI from online job postings, available
    with a two-week lag at monthly frequency, is the same measure used
    in \citet{azar2020} and our empirical section. A rising aggregate HHI
    (employment-weighted across markets) signals deepening monopsony as
    labor demand contracts, which the model maps to a larger markdown gap.
    The correlation between aggregate Lightcast HHI growth and
    $\hat\mu_t^w$ from the calibrated model over 2018--2023 is 0.79.

  \item \emph{Wage-productivity gap (ECI minus labor productivity).}
    The difference between the Employment Cost Index (wages and salaries
    component) and BLS labor productivity (output per hour) is the
    directly observable counterpart of the markdown: when wages fall
    relative to productivity, the gap $\mu_t^w$ deepens. This
    ``real-time markup proxy'' is available at quarterly frequency from
    the BLS with a four-week lag. Its correlation with $\hat\mu_t^w$
    from the calibrated model is 0.72.
\end{enumerate}

Combining both proxies with weights proportional to their model
correlations (0.79 and 0.72) yields a composite that captures 83\% of
$\hat\mu_t^w$ variance in the calibration sample. This converts
the theoretically optimal augmented rule into an implementable real-time
decision rule that requires no structural model estimation at the
meeting-by-meeting horizon.

\subsection{The Augmented Rule as a Normative Benchmark}
\label{sec:normative}

The augmented Taylor rule \eqref{eq:optimal_rule} is best understood as
a \emph{normative welfare benchmark}, not an immediate operational directive.
Two qualifications are in order.

\paragraph{The Lucas Critique.}
If a central bank announces an explicit commitment to respond to the wage
markdown $\hat\mu_t^w$, firms that currently set wages monopsonistically
may alter their behavior in anticipation of accommodation, changing the
structural parameter $\gamma$ that the rule is calibrated to. The welfare
analysis in Proposition~3 holds under the assumption that $\gamma$ is
policy-invariant---a standard hypothesis in the structural estimation
literature but one that breaks down if the augmented rule is communicated
as a firm commitment rather than a conditional benchmark. We therefore
recommend interpreting the rule as follows: the central bank uses the
markdown and fragmentation proxies from Section~\ref{sec:observables}
as \emph{real-time diagnostic indicators} of the supply-side origin of
inflation, and the augmented coefficients as a welfare-consistent reference
for how much accommodation is justified, not as a mechanical commitment
to respond to any measured change in $\hat\mu_t^w$.

\paragraph{Practical implementation.}
In practice, the augmented rule's value lies primarily in the $\phi_\phi^*
= -0.22$ coefficient: the prescription that supply-side cost-push shocks
should not be fought with rate increases, because doing so adds output
sacrifice without reducing the supply-side origin of inflation. This
prescription is robust to the Lucas Critique because it concerns the
\emph{sign} of the response to an observable supply shock, not the precise
magnitude of the markdown response. The qualitative conclusion---do not
overtighten into a fragmentation-driven inflation episode---holds regardless
of whether $\gamma$ shifts post-announcement.

\subsection{Complementarity with Structural Policy}

The augmented Taylor rule is a second-best instrument. Antitrust enforcement
that raises $\bar\varepsilon$ steepens $\kappa^{mono}$ toward $\kappa^{comp}$,
making each rate hike more effective and reducing the sacrifice ratio.
Industrial policy that builds domestic upstream capacity lowers $\bar\phi$
and reduces the variance of fragmentation shocks the central bank must
absorb. The welfare optimum requires both: the central bank conditioning on
the current structural state through the augmented rule, and structural
policy investing at the frequency of long-run supply chain realignment.
Detailed welfare quantification of these complementarities is in the
Supplemental Appendix.
