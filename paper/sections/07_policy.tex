\section{Policy Implications}
\label{sec:policy}

\subsection{The Augmented Taylor Rule: Operational Implementation}

Proposition~3 prescribes two additions to the standard Taylor rule.

\paragraph{Accommodate the fragmentation premium ($\phi_\phi^* = -0.22$).}
When $\hat\phi_t > 0$, the economy faces a supply-side cost-push shock.
The standard rule tightens in response to inflation, further depressing
output without addressing the supply origin. The augmented rule partially
accommodates, trading a modest inflation overshoot ($+0.3$pp peak) for a
substantially smaller output trough ($-0.8$pp vs.\ $-1.4$pp). Operationally,
this coefficient can be conditioned on cross-bloc trade flow divergence,
shipping cost indices, or the composite fragmentation index $F_t$ from
Section~\ref{sec:data}. The welfare gain is 0.06\% at baseline
$\sigma_\phi = 0.05$, rising to $\approx 1\%$ at $\sigma_\phi = 0.20$.

\paragraph{Respond to the wage markdown indicator ($\phi_\mu^* \geq 0$).}
Markdown deepening in recession compresses incomes of hand-to-mouth
households, implying the central bank should ease more aggressively during
concentrated-market downturns. At baseline $\gamma = 0.8$, $\phi_\mu^*
\approx 0$; it rises to economically significant magnitudes as markdown
cyclicality or the HtM share increase. Conditioned on HHI data from
\citet{azar2020}, this provides a real-time operational handle on
distributional monetary policy.

\paragraph{Revising the NAIRU.}
The wage-consistent NAIRU lies below the inflation-consistent NAIRU by:
\begin{equation}
  u^*_{\text{price}} - u^*_{\text{wage}} = \frac{\bar\mu^w}{\gamma}
  \left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_p} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_w}\right] \approx 0.4\;\text{pp}
  \label{eq:nairu_gap}
\end{equation}
Central banks using wage-consistent NAIRU estimates may systematically
overtighten, generating unnecessary unemployment.

\subsection{Complementarity with Structural Policy}

The augmented Taylor rule is a second-best instrument. Antitrust enforcement
that raises $\bar\varepsilon$ steepens $\kappa^{mono}$ toward $\kappa^{comp}$,
making each rate hike more effective and reducing the sacrifice ratio.
Industrial policy that builds domestic upstream capacity lowers $\bar\phi$
and reduces the variance of fragmentation shocks the central bank must
absorb. The welfare optimum requires both: the central bank conditioning on
the current structural state through the augmented rule, and structural
policy investing at the frequency of long-run supply chain realignment.
Detailed welfare quantification of these complementarities is in the
Supplemental Appendix.
