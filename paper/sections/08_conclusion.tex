\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We build and estimate a quantitative New Keynesian model in which two features
of the modern economy---structural monopsony and geoeconomic fragmentation---
interact non-linearly through the input-output network and jointly determine
monetary policy transmission. The central finding is that fragmentation
concentrates Domar weight in exactly those upstream sectors where monopsony
is most severe, generating wage suppression that is 3.4 times larger in
goods sectors than services per unit of trade disruption. Neither the
IO channel nor the monopsony channel alone produces this cross-sectional
pattern; both are empirically necessary, as confirmed by the
commuting-zone Bartik design ($\hat\beta_3 < 0$, predicted magnitude
$\approx -0.18$ pp per SD interaction) and by the 2018--2019 US--China
trade-war historical analogue.

Three quantitative results guide policy. The monopsony-adjusted NKPC slope
($\kappa^{mono} = 0.307$) implies a sacrifice ratio 1.5\% above the
competitive benchmark, a difference that compounds across the cross-section:
concentrated industries bear disproportionate real wage compression, and
the Bartik evidence confirms this distributional gradient. The TFP cost of
fragmentation ($-0.47\%$ per 15pp shock) provides a structural formula
linking supply chain policy to monetary policy effectiveness. And the
optimal augmented Taylor rule ($\phi_\phi^* = -0.22$) quantifies precisely
how much accommodation is warranted when stagflationary supply shocks
originate from geopolitical decoupling rather than domestic demand---the
defining challenge of the post-2021 policy environment.

Three limitations point toward future work. First, the 10-sector IO
aggregation is conservative; finer sector disaggregation (e.g., BEA
400+ sector table or firm-to-firm transaction data) would identify
Domar weight concentrations within, not only across, our sectors and
likely produce larger cross-sectional wage dispersion. Second, the key
structural parameter $\gamma$ is calibrated and micro-estimated but not
structurally estimated via SMM or Bayesian methods on the full model; doing
so would quantify uncertainty around the Phillips curve flattening and
sacrifice ratio estimates. Third, the fragmentation premium $\phi_t$ is
modeled as exogenous; endogenizing it via a strategic trade-policy game
(sketched in the Supplemental Appendix) would allow the model to speak
to the equilibrium level of geopolitical fragmentation and its interaction
with optimal monetary policy design.
