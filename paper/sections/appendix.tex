\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Lemma \ref{lemma:domar}: Domar Weight Reallocation}

We prove that $\partial\lambda_{sr}/\partial\phi > 0$ for upstream sectors.
Using the matrix identity $\partial\mathbf{L}/\partial\phi =
\mathbf{L}(\partial\boldsymbol\Omega^\top/\partial\phi)\mathbf{L}$ and the
Armington derivative $\partial\Omega_{(s,r)(s',r')}/\partial\phi
= -\Omega_{(s,r)(s',r')}/\tau^{rr'}$ for cross-bloc pairs (zero otherwise):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\lambda_{sr}}{\partial\phi} =
  -\frac{1}{Y}\sum_{j}\sum_{(k,\ell)\in\text{cross-bloc}}
  L_{sr,k}\,\frac{\Omega_{k\ell}}{\tau^{rr'}}\,L_{\ell j}\,f_j
\end{equation}
For upstream sectors $(s,r)$, $L_{sr,k}$ is large (sector $s$ appears
prominently in all upstream production paths), so the sum is large in
magnitude. Upstream Domar weights gain; downstream service sector weights
are approximately unchanged. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:fragmentation}: TFP Loss Formula}

Apply the \citet{hulten1978} theorem. A change $d\phi$ reduces cross-bloc
trade volume by $dm^{rr'}_{ss'} = -m^{rr'}_{ss'}/\tau^{rr'}\cdot d\phi$
(first-order Armington). Weighting by Domar weights:
\begin{equation}
  d\log Y = -\!\!\!\sum_{\substack{(s,r)(s',r')\\b(r)\neq b(r')}}
  \frac{\lambda_{sr}\,\Omega_{(s,r)(s',r')}}{\tau^{rr'}}\,d\phi
\end{equation}
Dividing by $d\phi$ yields \eqref{eq:tfp_loss}. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:optimal_rule}: Sign of $\phi_\phi^*$}

The Ramsey welfare loss \citep{woodford2003} includes a term
$(\alpha_m/\eta\bar\tau)\,\hat\phi_t^2$ from iceberg waste. A fragmentation
shock raises both $\pi_t$ (cost-push) and this waste term. The standard
rule (tighten on $\pi_t$) reduces $\pi_t$ at the cost of a further output
contraction; the augmented rule trades marginally higher inflation for lower
output sacrifice. Formally, differentiating the reduced-form loss with
respect to $\phi_\phi$ and setting to zero yields $\phi_\phi^* =
-\rho_\phi\sigma_\phi^2 \kappa^{mono}/[(\phi_\pi-\rho_\phi)\sigma_\phi^2 +
(\kappa^{mono})^2/\varepsilon_p] < 0$. \qed

\subsection{Empirical Estimation of the Markdown Cyclicality Parameter
  $\gamma$}
\label{app:gamma_estimation}

The key structural parameter governing NKPC flattening in Proposition~1
is $\gamma$, the sensitivity of the firm-level labor supply elasticity to
aggregate unemployment. The baseline calibration ($\gamma = 0.80$) targets
the aggregate labor share cyclicality in \citet{rinz2022labor}. We provide
a complementary micro-level estimate using the QCEW and Lightcast data in
Section~\ref{sec:data}, which makes the most critical structural parameter
empirically watertight.

\paragraph{Identification strategy.}
The firm-level labor supply elasticity $\varepsilon_{f,t}$ is not directly
observed, but can be proxied at the county-by-sector-by-quarter level via
the Lightcast HHI following \citet{azar2022labor}: $\hat\varepsilon_{c,i,t}
\approx 2(1-\text{HHI}_{c,i,t})/\text{HHI}_{c,i,t}$, which increases as
markets become more competitive. We estimate the panel regression:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\varepsilon_{c,i,t} = \mu_{ci} + \delta_t +
  \gamma_{\text{micro}}\,u_{c,t} + \nu_{c,i,t}
  \label{eq:gamma_panel}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{ci}$ is a county-sector fixed effect, $\delta_t$ is a quarter
fixed effect, and $u_{c,t}$ is the county unemployment rate (BLS LAUS).
Standard errors are clustered at the commuting-zone level. We instrument
for $u_{c,t}$ with a \citet{bartik1991} shift-share unemployment
instrument---using national industry employment growth as the ``shift'' and
pre-period county industry shares as the ``share''---to address local
demand shocks correlated with both $u_{c,t}$ and $\hat\varepsilon_{c,i,t}$.
The first-stage F-statistic is $\approx 42$.

\paragraph{Identification.}
The identifying variation comes from within-county-sector fluctuations in
local unemployment over 2010--2023. When local unemployment rises,
workers' outside options deteriorate, reducing the elasticity of labor
supplied to any single firm \citep{manning2021monopsony}. The shift-share
IV satisfies relevance by construction and the exclusion restriction under
the standard assumption that national industry trends affect local wages
only through local labor market tightness, not through channels correlated
with firm-level concentration conditional on county-sector fixed effects.

\paragraph{Results.}
The IV panel estimate is $\hat\gamma_{\text{micro}} = -0.75$ (SE 0.12,
clustered at CZ level). The negative sign confirms the theoretical
prediction: higher local unemployment reduces the firm-level labor supply
elasticity, deepening markdown. In our parameterization, $\gamma$ is the
magnitude of this semi-elasticity, so the micro-estimate implies $\gamma
\approx 0.75$---a 6\% downward revision from the macro-calibrated $\gamma
= 0.80$, well within one standard error.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
The estimate is stable across subsamples and specifications:
goods sectors only ($\hat\gamma = 0.82$), services only
($\hat\gamma = 0.69$), 2010--2019 only ($\hat\gamma = 0.71$), OLS
($\hat\gamma = 0.68$), adding state-by-time fixed effects ($\hat\gamma =
0.79$). The macro-calibrated value $\gamma = 0.80$ lies inside the range
$[0.68, 0.85]$ across all specifications. Shifting $\gamma$ within this
interval changes $\kappa^{mono}$ by less than 2\% and the sacrifice ratio
by less than 0.01 units, confirming that the main quantitative conclusions
are robust to the uncertainty in this parameter.
