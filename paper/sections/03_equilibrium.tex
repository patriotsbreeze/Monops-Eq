\section{Equilibrium and Log-Linearization}
\label{sec:equilibrium}

A sequential competitive equilibrium consists of household plans,
firm price and wage policies, and price sequences such that the household
maximizes \eqref{eq:hh_objective}--\eqref{eq:bc}, firms optimize
\eqref{eq:production} subject to monopsonistic labor supply
\eqref{eq:labor_supply} and Calvo pricing \eqref{eq:optimal_price}, and
all markets clear. Under monopsony, the labor market clears at
$w_t = \mu_t^w \cdot \mathrm{MPL}_t$---below the competitive wage---while
goods and asset markets clear in the standard fashion. The government runs
a balanced budget with constant bond supply $B_t = \bar B$.

\paragraph{Key log-linearized equations.}
The monopsony-adjusted Phillips curve (derived in Section~\ref{sec:results}):
\begin{equation}
  \pi_t = \beta\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1} + \kappa^{mono}\,\tilde{y}_t
  \label{eq:NKPC_linearized}
\end{equation}
The standard IS curve (log-linearized Euler equation):
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{y}_t = \mathbb{E}_t\tilde{y}_{t+1} -
  \sigma^{-1}(i_t - \mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1} - \bar r)
  \label{eq:IS_linearized}
\end{equation}
Fragmentation enters through the Phillips curve slope $\kappa^{mono}$
(which is state-dependent in $\phi_t$ via Domar weight reallocation) and
through the effective marginal cost of intermediates, which shifts the
NKPC intercept in the presence of supply shocks. The augmented Taylor rule
\eqref{eq:taylor} closes the system. Full market-clearing conditions and
the steady-state computation are in the Supplemental Appendix.
