We build a quantitative New Keynesian model with multi-sector input-output
networks and structural monopsony in labor markets. Three results emerge.
First, countercyclical wage markdowns endogenously flatten the Phillips curve
(slope falls 1.4\% in baseline); a two-sector analytical model makes the
IO$\times$Monopsony non-linearity transparent in closed form. Second,
geoeconomic fragmentation concentrates Domar weights in high-monopsony
upstream sectors, amplifying wage suppression non-linearly; a 15pp
fragmentation shock reduces aggregate TFP by 0.47\%. Third, the
welfare-optimal Taylor rule accommodates supply-side inflation
($\phi_\phi^* = -0.22$), not fights it. A commuting-zone Bartik design
confirms the IO$\times$Monopsony wage interaction in 2021--2023 microdata;
a 2018--2019 US--China trade-war analogue validates the mechanism in a
pre-COVID environment.
